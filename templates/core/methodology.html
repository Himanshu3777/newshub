Methodology

Data sources

Official RSS feeds and authorized APIs only. No paywall or CAPTCHA bypass. Only metadata and short summaries are stored.

Unconfirmed reports

Reports that come only from viral posts or videos are shown with a clear note that they are not confirmed. We never state them as fact.

Correction policy

Anyone can submit a correction. An editor reviews it, and every correction is logged and dated.

How we handle fake and social-media news

• Stories our verification finds false, misleading or disputed are removed from the home page, news feed, search and API automatically. They stay visible only on the Fact Check page, clearly marked, so readers can see what was flagged and why.

• News that starts on Instagram, WhatsApp, Facebook, X or YouTube is never published as confirmed news. If a publisher reports a claim that comes from a viral post, the story carries a source note until an official source or another publisher confirms it — then the note is removed automatically.

• Anyone can paste a viral post or forward into Check a Claim. We compare it with publisher reporting and existing fact-checks and answer: confirmed, not confirmed, or contradicted. Submissions are never turned into news articles.

How we write unconfirmed news

"According to a viral Instagram video, [claim]. However, this has not been confirmed."
  1. Always say where the claim came from (viral video, social post, unnamed source).
  2. Always say that it is not confirmed. Never write it as a fact.
  3. If a publisher or official source confirms it later, the note is removed and the story is updated.
